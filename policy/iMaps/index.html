iMaps is a community-powered navigation app. It shows a map, plans driving routes, lets people report road incidents, aggregates public hazard data near you, and lists public-safety radio streams. This policy describes exactly what the app does with information, based on the behaviour of the application code.

The short version. iMaps works without an account and without a name, phone number, or email. It does not track you in the background, does not read your contacts, camera, microphone, photos, or files, contains no crash-reporting or third-party tracking SDK, and does not sell personal information. Its main privacy consideration is that your location coordinates are sent to third-party mapping and public-safety APIs in order to draw the map and find things near you.

1. Information we collect

iMaps has no registration step and no user profile. The information involved falls into these categories.

2. What we do not collect

The application contains no code that accesses or transmits any of the following:

  • Your name, phone number, postal address, or date of birth. (If you sign in with Apple, Apple is asked for your name and email; see section 6.)
  • Contacts, call logs, SMS messages, or your calendar.
  • Camera, microphone, photos, media, or files on your device.
  • Biometric data, health or fitness data, or clipboard contents.
  • Your Android Advertising ID, Android ID, IMEI, MAC address, or phone number — the app itself never reads these. (See section 11 for what the bundled Google advertising library may access.)
  • Installed-app inventory, browsing history outside the app, or keystrokes.
  • Payment card numbers or billing details — these are handled entirely by Google Play and are never visible to the app.
  • A continuous record of your movements. The app keeps no location history.

3. Location

Location is the core of the app, and it is used only while you are actively using it.

What it is used for

  • Showing your position on the map and centring the view.
  • Planning driving routes and giving turn-by-turn directions.
  • Finding nearby community incident reports and alerting you to them.
  • Fetching public hazard data for roughly a 2-mile (3.22 km) radius around you — earthquakes, weather alerts, wildfires, disasters and municipal 311 reports.
  • Finding nearby EV chargers, fuel stations, parking, transit stops, speed limits and elevation.
  • Showing local weather and selecting relevant public-safety radio streams.
  • Choosing which regional partner offers to display.

How it is shared

To provide those features, your coordinates are sent to the third-party services listed in section 13. Each request is made directly from your device to that service over HTTPS. We do not build a location profile, and your coordinates are not stored on our servers unless they form part of an incident report you submit.

Precision matters here. Several public data sources receive your coordinates in order to answer "what is near me?" — including the United States Geological Survey, the National Weather Service, NASA FIRMS, OpenStreetMap Overpass, Open-Meteo, OpenAQ and, in five US cities, that city's 311 system. Those organisations handle that data under their own privacy policies, which we do not control.

Declining location

Location permission is requested at runtime and you may refuse it. If you do, the app still runs: it falls back to a regional default map position, and features that depend on proximity will be limited or empty. You can change or revoke the permission at any time in Android Settings → Apps → iMaps → Permissions.

4. Background location

iMaps does not collect location in the background. The app does not request the ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION permission, does not declare a location foreground service, and runs no location tracking when it is not in the foreground. Location access stops when you leave the app.

5. Incident reports

Reporting is entirely optional and requires no account. When you submit a report, it is saved on your device immediately and then sent to the iMaps backend so other users can see it. A report contains the incident type, its coordinates, an optional description you type, an optional street name, the time, and the random device identifier from section 1.

Reports are public. A report you submit is shared with other users of the app, including any description you write. Please do not type names, licence plates, phone numbers, or any other personal information about yourself or anyone else into the description field.

The random device identifier lets the service attribute and moderate reports. It is not linked to your name or email, and it is not linked to your sign-in account.

6. Accounts and sign-in

Signing in is optional. The app opens directly into guest mode and never blocks any feature behind a login screen. Sign-in is reachable only from Settings → Account, and exists to support optional cloud sync.

Authentication is provided by Supabase. If you choose to sign in, these options exist:

  • Email and password — your email address is sent to Supabase and stored on your device so the session persists.
  • Sign in with Google — the Google Sign-In SDK returns an identity token which is exchanged with Supabase. Google shares your email address with us as part of this.
  • Sign in with Apple — the app requests the email and full name scopes from Apple. Apple lets you hide your real address using its private relay service.
  • Anonymous sign-in — creates a Supabase session with no personal information at all.

The only personal information retained from sign-in is your email address (plus a name, if Apple provides one). Signing out clears the session from your device and returns you to guest mode.

7. Notifications

All notifications are generated locally on your device. There is no push-notification service, no push token, and no server that can send you a message. Nothing about notifications leaves your device.

When the app is open and detects a reported incident near you, it raises a local Android notification. You control this through the app's own alert toggles and through the Android notification permission, which you may deny or revoke at any time.

9. Analytics

iMaps uses no third-party analytics product. There is no Google Analytics, Firebase, Mixpanel, Amplitude, Segment, AppsFlyer, Adjust, Facebook SDK, or comparable service in the app. Analytics are first-party and deliberately minimal: a fixed set of five events, batched and sent to the iMaps backend.

10. Crash reporting

iMaps contains no crash-reporting or diagnostics SDK. There is no Crashlytics, Sentry, Bugsnag, or equivalent. The app does not transmit stack traces, device logs, or performance traces to us.

Separately, if you have opted in to Google Play's own crash reporting at the Android system level, Google may share aggregated crash data with us as the developer. That collection is performed by Android, not by iMaps, and is governed by Google's privacy policy.

11. Advertising

iMaps includes the Google Mobile Ads library. We want to be precise about its current state:

  • The library is initialised when the app starts, and it ships the Android advertising-identifier permissions (com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID and the Android Privacy Sandbox ACCESS_ADSERVICES_* permissions). On initialisation, Google's library may access your device's Advertising ID and related device signals under Google's own policies.
  • The current release does not display any advertisement. No banner, native, interstitial, or rewarded ad unit is requested or rendered anywhere in the app.

If a future version begins showing ads, this policy will be updated before that version is published. You can reset or delete your Advertising ID at any time in Android Settings → Privacy → Ads. See how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

The app also shows its own partner offers, which are not served by an ad network. These are a fixed list compiled into the app and selected by broad region; opening one sends you to that company's website in your browser with a referral tag, and we may earn a commission. No personal information is transmitted to the partner by the app itself.

12. Purchases

iMaps offers optional premium subscriptions. All payment processing is performed by Google Play Billing. The app never sees or handles your payment card, billing address, or Google account credentials.

To confirm a purchase is genuine, the app sends the platform name, the product identifier, and the signed receipt issued by Google Play to the iMaps backend for verification. Your entitlement is then recorded on your device. Subscription management and cancellation are handled in your Google Play account.

16. External links

Some actions open a third-party website or app outside iMaps:

  • Partner offers — open a retail or travel website in your browser, with a referral tag identifying iMaps as the referrer.
  • Radio streams — open OpenMHz, Broadcastify or LiveATC in your browser or media player.
  • Suggestion cards — may open a partner website.

Once you leave the app, the destination site's own privacy policy and cookie practices apply. We have no control over, and take no responsibility for, the content or data practices of those sites.

17. Cookies

The Android application does not use cookies and contains no embedded advertising or tracking web view.

This privacy policy page is static and sets no cookies of its own. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, and GitHub may log requests, including IP addresses, as described in the GitHub Privacy Statement.

A browser-based build of iMaps also exists. If you use it and choose to sign in, the Supabase authentication library stores your session in your browser's local storage so you stay signed in. That build is not the Google Play application described by this policy.

18. Retention

  • On your device — preferences and identifiers persist until you clear the app's data or uninstall it. Cached incident reports are discarded once they are more than 2 hours old.
  • Incident reports — retained on the backend for as long as needed to operate and moderate the community reporting feature. Reports age out of the app's active view automatically.
  • Analytics events — retained in aggregate to understand feature use. They contain no location and no directly identifying information.
  • Account data — if you signed in, your email address is retained by our authentication provider until the account is deleted.

19. Your rights and choices

Controls available immediately

  • Revoke location at any time in Android app permissions; the app continues to function.
  • Turn off alerts in the app's settings or revoke the notification permission.
  • Stay a guest — never sign in, and no email is ever collected.
  • Reset all identifiers by clearing the app's data or uninstalling. New random identifiers are generated on next use, with no link to the previous ones.
  • Reset your Advertising ID in Android privacy settings.

Legal rights

Depending on where you live — including under the EU and UK GDPR and the California Consumer Privacy Act — you may have the right to access, correct, export, or delete personal information we hold about you, to object to or restrict its processing, and not to be discriminated against for exercising those rights. We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising.

To exercise a right, email contact@safecodeg.com. Because the app is usable without an account, we may be unable to locate device-level data without the relevant identifier; if you have signed in, please write from the address associated with your account so we can verify the request. We respond to verified requests within the period required by applicable law.

Where we rely on consent — for location access and notifications — you may withdraw it at any time through Android's permission controls.

International transfers

The services described in section 13 operate in multiple countries, including the United States. Using iMaps involves transferring the data described above to those services, which may be located outside your country of residence.

20. Children

iMaps is a driving and navigation aid intended for adults. It is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has provided personal information through the app, contact us and we will delete it.

21. Security

  • All network traffic uses HTTPS. Cleartext HTTP is disabled in the app's Android network-security configuration, and the app trusts only the system certificate store.
  • Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer are disabled for the app, so saved sessions and identifiers are not copied off your device by the operating system.
  • Release builds are code-shrunk and resource-shrunk, and the Android code layer is obfuscated (R8).
  • Purchases are verified server-side; entitlements are never granted on the basis of an unverified client claim.

No method of transmission or storage is completely secure, and we cannot guarantee absolute security.
